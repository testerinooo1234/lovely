# Where to Live in the U.S. — Deep Research Against Your Priorities

_Prepared as a multi-agent research effort. All climate figures are long-term
station normals (NOAA / Current Results / Extreme Weather Watch); all housing
figures are 2026 market data (Redfin / Realtor.com / local MLS reports).
Sources are linked throughout._

---

## Your priorities (as given)

**Must be met 100% (your words):**
1. Home under **$800,000**
2. Over **1,750 sq ft**
3. **Very rarely over 80 °F**
4. Within **~1 hour of a major metro**

**Strongly desired:**
5. An **MLS or MLB** team nearby
6. **Not overly conservative**
7. Over **2,000 sq ft** (a stronger version of #2)
7. **Low or no state income tax**

---

## Bottom line up front

**No location in the United States satisfies priorities 1, 2, and 3 at the same
time within an hour of a major metro.** They are mutually exclusive in today's
market, and here is exactly why:

- Priority **#3 ("very rarely over 80 °F")** is the single most restrictive
  climate constraint in the country. Only the **cool, marine-influenced
  California coast** clears it near a major metro — chiefly **San Francisco and
  its immediate coastal suburbs** (and, more loosely, San Diego's coast).
- In precisely those places, home prices run **$470 – $1,000+ per square foot**.
  At those prices, **$800,000 buys roughly 800 – 1,700 sq ft**, not 1,750 – 2,000+.

The math that kills it:
- To hit **#1 + #2** you need **≤ $457/sq ft** ($800,000 ÷ 1,750).
- To hit **#1 + #7-size** you need **≤ $400/sq ft** ($800,000 ÷ 2,000).
- **Every "very-rarely-over-80" location is above $457/sq ft.** The cheapest one
  (coastal San Diego / Chula Vista, ~$470/sq ft) is *still over the line* — and
  its climate is only "mild," not "very rarely over 80."

So you have to decide **which must-have to bend.** The rest of this report shows
the data and lays out the three coherent strategies, then gives a single
recommendation.

## Three coherent strategies (pick which must-have to bend)

### Strategy 1 — Protect the climate (#3); accept a smaller / attached home
**Where:** Coastal San Francisco — the city itself, **Daly City, Pacifica, South SF, San Bruno.**

| Priority | Result |
|---|---|
| #1 Under $800k | ✅ possible — but only a **~900–1,100 sq ft condo/small house** |
| #2 Over 1,750 sq ft | ❌ ~$1.5M at this size — not achievable under $800k |
| #3 Very rarely > 80 °F | ✅✅ **Best in the U.S.** (18 days/yr in SF; fewer on the coast; nights always < 70 °F) |
| #4 Within 1 hr of metro | ✅ inside the SF metro |
| #5 MLB/MLS | ✅ SF **Giants** (MLB) + San Jose **Earthquakes** (MLS) |
| #6 Not conservative | ✅ strongly liberal |
| #7 Low/no income tax | ❌ California up to **13.3%** |

Verdict: The only way to keep your **#3** perfectly is to give up your **#2/#7
size** and accept a condo/townhome, plus high CA taxes.

### Strategy 2 — Protect budget + size + metro + tax; accept "mild" instead of "rare" ⭐ recommended
**Where:** **Seattle metro suburbs — Tacoma, Everett, Bremerton/Kitsap County.**

| Priority | Result |
|---|---|
| #1 Under $800k | ✅ easily (Tacoma median ~$500k) |
| #2 Over 1,750 sq ft | ✅ easily |
| #3 Very rarely > 80 °F | ⚠️ **Not literally** — ~31 days/yr > 80 °F. **But** July highs are only 77–79 °F, just ~4 days/yr reach 90 °F, and nights cool to ~57–58 °F. Genuinely mild, if not "rare." |
| #4 Within 1 hr of metro | ✅ within ~1 hr of Seattle |
| #5 MLB/MLS | ✅ **Mariners** (MLB) + **Sounders** (MLS) |
| #6 Not conservative | ✅ King County strongly Dem; Pierce (Tacoma) & Kitsap lean blue |
| #7 Over 2,000 sq ft | ✅ ~$670–790k for 2,000 sq ft |
| #7 Low/no income tax | ✅ **Washington has NO state income tax** (only a 7% capital-gains *excise* on long-term gains above ~$262k/yr; **real estate gains are exempt**) — [WA DOR](https://www.dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/other-taxes/capital-gains-tax) |

Verdict: **Satisfies 7 of your 8 priorities in full**, missing only the *literal*
wording of #3 — and it's the **only** path that keeps a 2,000+ sq ft house under
$800k while adding no income tax and both an MLB and MLS team.

| Priority | Result |

Verdict: Nice climate-and-sports blend near a metro, but it **breaks the
2,000 sq ft goal, is tight on the $800k/1,750 sq ft line, and keeps high CA taxes.**

## Why the "obvious cheap + cool" options don't qualify

- **Portland, OR** — cheap and liberal with the **Timbers** (MLS), but summers are
  hot: **63 days/yr > 80 °F, ~16 days > 90 °F**, July high 82–83 °F, and warming.
  Fails #3. (Oregon also has income tax up to ~9.9%.)
- **Eureka/Arcata (Humboldt), coastal Oregon, Bellingham WA** — genuinely cool
  (rarely > 80 °F) **and** cheap with large homes, **but none is within an hour of
  a major metro.** Fails #4.
- **Great Lakes / Midwest / Northeast / South** (Buffalo, Cleveland, Duluth,
  coastal New England, etc.) — hot, humid summers with 40–90+ days/yr over 80 °F.
  Fail #3.

## Single recommendation

Because priorities **1, 2, and 3 cannot all be true at once near a U.S. metro**,
the best real-world answer depends on which one is truly inviolable:

- **If "very rarely over 80 °F" is absolutely non-negotiable in the strict sense:**
  the honest answer is **coastal San Francisco / Daly City / Pacifica**, buying a
  **condo or small home (~900–1,100 sq ft) under $800k** — you keep the perfect
  climate, the metro, the teams, and the politics, but give up the 1,750+ sq ft
  house and pay CA taxes. Nowhere lets you keep both the climate *and* the big
  cheap house.

- **If you can accept "mild summers with cool nights" in place of the literal
  rule (my recommendation):** go with the **Seattle metro — Tacoma, Kitsap
  County (Bremerton), or Everett.** It's the only option that fully delivers
  **budget, size (2,000+ sq ft), metro proximity, an MLB *and* MLS team, a
  not-conservative electorate, and zero state income tax** — sacrificing only the
  strict version of #3 (about a month of 80 °F+ days, but highs rarely above the
  low 80s and nights in the upper 50s).
