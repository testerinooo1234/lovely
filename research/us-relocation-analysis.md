_Prepared as a multi-agent research effort. All climate figures are long-term

## Your priorities (as given)

**Must be met 100% (your words):**
1. Home under **$800,000**
2. Over **1,750 sq ft**
3. **Very rarely over 80 °F**
4. Within **~1 hour of a major metro**

**Strongly desired:**
5. An **MLS or MLB** team nearby
6. **Not overly conservative**
7. Over **2,000 sq ft** (a stronger version of #2)
7. **Low or no state income tax**

---

## Bottom line up front

Priorities 1–4 are **extremely** hard to satisfy together, because #3 ("very
rarely over 80 °F") confines you to the cool, marine-influenced California coast,
where home prices normally run **$470 – $1,000+ per square foot** — so $800,000
usually buys only **800 – 1,700 sq ft**, not 1,750 – 2,000+.

**But there is one real exception that threads all four must-haves: Richmond, CA
(Contra Costa County), on the northeast shore of San Francisco Bay.** It is
genuinely marine-cooled (July avg high ~71 °F; only ~0.3 days/yr reach 90 °F),
its single-family median is only ~$700k, and **2,000+ sq ft houses under $800k do
exist** in its larger-home inland/hill ZIP codes (Hilltop 94806, El Sobrante
94803). It's ~30–45 min from San Francisco/Oakland, near the Giants (MLB), and in
a deep-blue county. The one thing it *can't* give you is the low-tax nice-to-have
(#7) — California has the highest income tax in the country — and the caveat is
that the affordable 2,000+ sq ft inventory sits in the **slightly warmer inland**
Richmond neighborhoods (a few degrees above the bayfront), so you must verify the
specific micro-location's summer temperatures on a given house.

The reason this is the *only* clean fit comes down to price-per-square-foot math:
- To hit **#1 + #2** you need **≤ $457/sq ft** ($800,000 ÷ 1,750).
- To hit **#1 + #7-size** you need **≤ $400/sq ft** ($800,000 ÷ 2,000).
- Almost every "very-rarely-over-80" location is far above $457/sq ft (SF ~$1,080;
  Daly City/Pacifica ~$850; Santa Cruz ~$787; coastal San Diego ~$470–590).
  **Richmond (~$409–530/sq ft) is the rare cool-climate market that dips low
  enough** — and even it only clears the 2,000 sq ft bar in its larger-home ZIPs.

So the decision is really: **take Richmond and accept high CA taxes**, or bend one
of the other priorities. The rest of this report lays out the data and the
alternative strategies, then gives a single recommendation.

## Why the "obvious cheap + cool" options don't qualify

- **Portland, OR** — cheap and liberal with the **Timbers** (MLS), but summers are
  hot: **63 days/yr > 80 °F, ~16 days > 90 °F**, July high 82–83 °F, and warming.
  Fails #3. (Oregon also has income tax up to ~9.9%.)
- **Eureka/Arcata (Humboldt), coastal Oregon, Bellingham WA** — genuinely cool
  (rarely > 80 °F) **and** cheap with large homes, **but none is within an hour of
  a major metro.** Fails #4.
- **Vallejo, CA** — the cheapest Bay Area market ($540k median, ~$367/sq ft →
  ~2,180 sq ft for $800k) and within ~1 hr of SF, but its **July/Aug average high
  is right at 80 °F**, so it exceeds 80 °F on a large share of summer afternoons.
  Fails #3 (this is the trade-off vs. cooler, pricier Richmond next door).
- **Great Lakes / Midwest / Northeast / South** (Buffalo, Cleveland, Duluth,
  coastal New England, etc.) — hot, humid summers with 40–90+ days/yr over 80 °F.
  Fail #3.

## Single recommendation

**Richmond, CA (Contra Costa County) is the best answer** — the one place that
plausibly satisfies **all four of your non-negotiable priorities at once**: a
2,000+ sq ft single-family home under $800k (in the inland/hill ZIPs 94806/94803),
a genuinely marine-cooled climate that very rarely tops 80 °F (July high ~71 °F,
~0.3 days/yr ≥ 90 °F), ~30–45 min from San Francisco/Oakland, plus the Giants
(MLB) and Earthquakes (MLS) and a deeply Democratic county. Its **only** miss is
the low-tax nice-to-have (California income tax up to 13.3%), and the one caveat is
to confirm summer temps at the specific inland address, since that's where the
larger sub-$800k homes are.

If that tax picture is a dealbreaker, the fallbacks are:

- **Want NO state income tax above all?** → **Seattle metro — Tacoma, Kitsap
  (Bremerton), or Everett.** Delivers budget, 2,000+ sq ft, metro proximity, an
  MLB *and* MLS team, a not-conservative electorate, and **zero income tax** —
  sacrificing only the strict version of #3 (~a month of 80 °F+ days, rising, but
  highs rarely above the low 80s and cool nights).

- **Want the coolest possible summer, period?** → **coastal San Francisco /
  Daly City / Pacifica**, buying a **condo or small home (~900–1,100 sq ft) under
  $800k** — the best climate in the country, but you give up the 1,750+ sq ft
  house and still pay CA taxes.

- **Want a warm-but-mild coast with an MLB + brand-new MLS club?** → **coastal-
  adjacent San Diego (National City, western Chula Vista 91911, inland
  Oceanside)** — near the metro with the Padres + San Diego FC, but 2,000 sq ft
  runs ~$940k and CA taxes are high.
